# Terminal card

What this covers: how a terminal card draws and behaves: the cell, fonts and glyph budget, text selection and copy, the cursor, links and issue references, visual mode, the font-size keys, and the zsh command-line selection.

Read it before touching `infiniterm-ui/src/terminal_body.rs`, `terminals.rs`, `infiniterm-term/src/grid.rs`, `visual_keys.rs`, `links.rs`, or `shell/infiniterm.zsh`.

Related: `agents-and-state.md` (a card's state colour, program marks and the zsh shim that sends them), `daemon-and-backends.md` (the pane under the body, replay, kitty keys), `traps-code.md` (paint, font and input traps).

Moved here from CLAUDE.md on 2026-10-19 with the text unchanged.

- The terminal cell is the advance of `M` at the configured weight; `letterSpacing` is ignored. Wide characters put `SPACER` in their second cell. Every terminal font carries the bundled `Symbols Nerd Font Mono` (`assets/fonts/`, MIT) as its fallback (`terminal_body::term_font`, named by its PostScript name `SymbolsNFM`), registered with CoreText itself by `icon_font.rs` (`CTFontManagerRegisterGraphicsFont`) because gpui resolves a fallback through CoreText by name and a font given to gpui's `add_fonts` is invisible there (the first try, 912373c, still drew boxes on the Air; 1563c47 was checked there with a screenshot of the prompt, 2026-09-24), because Nerd Font icons live in the private use area and CoreText does not borrow an installed font for it: a card in Menlo drew boxes for the prompt's icons on the MacBook Air, which had six Nerd Fonts installed (2026-09-24); WezTerm ships the same font for the same reason. A terminal row is shaped ONCE as one line and every glyph is painted at ITS CELL (`terminal_body::paint_row`: `cell_w * col` plus the glyph's offset from the first glyph of its own character, so combining marks and ligatures keep their shape); the shaper's own advances are never used for placement, which is what removed both the drift and the fifteen `shape_line` calls a Claude row used to cost. The zero-width characters alacritty keeps on a cell (U+FE0F, the ZWJ, combining accents) ride beside the row's text in `Row::zerowidth`, never in it, because the bar painter, the backgrounds and the link underlines count one character per column; `shape_row` puts them after their cell's character in the shaped text and on its slot, so ⚠️ shapes as the colour emoji and 🏃‍♀️ fuses (the wide cell's spacer never enters the shaped text). Before 2026-09-24 they were dropped, and ⚠️ drew as a flat ⚠. A fused ZWJ emoji is still three cells in alacritty's grid where the program counted two: the glyph covers the first two and the third stays blank. A body keeps its `Frame` and `Grid::update_frame` rebuilds only the rows alacritty damaged; links are rescanned for those rows only. Below the legible line text is drawn as bars; the line is `chrome::legible_font_px(scale_factor)`, 7 DEVICE pixels (enough to draw a glyph) but never under `LEGIBLE_FLOOR_PX` 4.5 logical (big enough to read), so 7 px on a 1x screen and 4.5 on Retina (2026-09-24: the fixed 7 px turned a card on the MacBook Air to bars at 48% while its text was 13 device pixels). The history of the number: below a 5 px font, text was drawn as bars (was 3; at 4 px nothing was readable and eleven cards at fit-all cost 85 ms a frame in glyphs, measured 2026-09-19: gpui's `paint_glyph` is 1.7 µs of CPU each and a frame repaints every card). Since 2026-09-24 there is ONE rule past legibility, the frame's glyph budget (`ui.glyphBudget`, `config::DEFAULT_GLYPH_BUDGET`, 150,000 cells since 2026-10-09 (30,000 before); a card's rows are painted inside `Window::paint_layer` (`terminal_body.rs`), which gives them one order and skips gpui's bounds tree, 90% of a glyph's cost: 17.5 ms to 4 ms a frame on 13 busy cards, 215 to 44 ns a glyph; the figures below that say 1.7 microseconds are the 2026-09-19 ones, about two full cards, or 1.5 windowfuls at 100% when that is more, `GLYPH_BUDGET_WINDOWS`: the fixed number was less than one windowful on the 4K, and three big cards covering the screen drew bars at 98%, 2026-09-24; the windowful is measured from the densest visible card): `paint_world` sums `CardBody::text_cells` over the visible cards, each weighted by the fraction of it the window shows, and over the budget every card draws bars (`set_crowded`) and content frames are rationed to one per `FAR_REFRESH_MS` (`AppView::far` is `crowded`); the terminal painter skips rows outside its content mask (`row_on_screen`), which is what makes a half-shown card cost half. The cost is the total, since a card's GRID does not shrink when the canvas does. Before, a font-size rule (`FAR_FONT_PX`, 12 px) drew bars while the viewport moved and rationed frames at rest by size alone; it stood in for the budget before the budget existed (2026-09-22), and it turned a single readable card at 57% (10.8 px, a smaller window on Ekin's 4K) into bars at 4 fps. Counted from the grids, not their contents, so the flag does not flip as output scrolls; only terminal bodies report cells so far. Rendering a card's text to a texture was checked and not done: gpui keeps its glyph rasteriser `pub(crate)`, so it would mean our own font path.
- A text selection is alacritty's, in grid points, painted in the app's selection pair. Its TEXT is ours (`Grid::selection_text`): Claude Code writes every screen row with a hard newline (checked in a Claude card's ring: no printable run longer than a row), so a copy across a wrapped paragraph came out broken mid-sentence; the WIDTH is the text's own (the widest row within 20 rows of the selection, because Claude wraps at the width it had when it drew, 73 columns on a 150-column card), at least two rows must reach it (one long row among short ones is the longest line, not a wrap) and it must be 40 or more (shorter rows are a listing); then a row within `SOFT_WRAP_SLACK` (12) of it whose next row continues at the same indent is joined with a space, the wrap indent dropped. A heuristic, said so. A body that starts a drag says so (`captures_drag`) and gets the moves and the release wherever the pointer goes. Shift+click extends the selection to the click (every Mac text view's rule) and the drag that follows keeps extending; a drag held past the card's top or bottom edge scrolls the grid a few lines a frame (`TerminalBody::autoscroll`, applied in `paint`, faster the further out, capped at `AUTOSCROLL_MAX_LINES`) with the selection's end following to the edge row.
- The cursor blinks from the frame clock; `wants_frame(now)` reports the next flip.
- Issue references (#335, 2026-10-09): `links::find_links_in(line, repo)` also finds `owner/repo#N` anywhere and a bare `#N` when the card's directory has a github.com remote (`git_repo::github_repo`, read from `.git/config` and cached per cwd in `TerminalBody::repo`, no `git` process); they win over the path or address they overlap. OSC 8 hyperlinks (#335): `Grid::build_row` reports each span as `Row::hyperlinks` (start column, end column, address; a blank inside a span is not skipped by the blank fast path), and `links::merge_hyperlinks` turns them into links that win over what they overlap, http and https only. A link the program marked is underlined at rest (`Row::hyperlinks` drawn as a thin dim line); pattern-found links show only under Cmd. Cmd over any link in a terminal is the hand pointer, and `browsers.rs` asks for it through `TerminalBody::link_hovered` because the window cursor is one global, not per element.
- The letter under a block cursor (#343, 2026-10-10): the terminal paints the block UNDER the text, so a theme whose cursor equals its foreground hid the letter; `with_cursor_cell` splits the cursor's cell out of its run with the cursor text colour on the cursor colour (the bg also covers a cell's own background quad, which painted over the block). The editor's caret is a 0.85-alpha block OVER the text, so the letter is shaped again on top in `EditorColors::cursor_text` (a line with a tab keeps the show-through: its columns are expanded). The colour is `label_colors::cursor_text_on`: the theme's `Cursor Text Color` when it has 3:1 contrast against the cursor, else black or white; a user cursor colour (`terminal.cursorColor`, `editor.cursorColor`) brings no accent of its own. Right-click on a card of a multi-selection always opens the Frame menu (#347), whose single-card rows (Rename, Split, Size) carry `show: "!multiSelection"`. `group.moveToWorkspace` (#348, `Source::MoveGroupTo`, `Model::move_group_to`) moves the focused card's whole group, frame pad included, rigidly into the destination's first free block slot; the group's own menu is not built.
- Cmd+= / Cmd+- are the terminal font (`terminal.font.bigger` / `.smaller`, 2026-09-28, Ekin), one point a press, clamped as `config.rs` clamps `terminal.fontSize`, set on `Model::config` so every terminal refits the next frame (the cell is measured from the config each frame) and saved through `Effect::SaveSetting`. They were the canvas zoom until then; the canvas keeps Cmd+scroll, the pinch, Cmd+0 and the palette. A locked browser still gives them to the page.
- Visual mode (Cmd+Shift+C, `terminal.visual`, 2026-09-27) is alacritty's vi mode under our keys: `Grid::visual_enter` / `visual_move` / `visual_select` / `visual_leave` in grid.rs, the key table in `infiniterm-term/src/visual_keys.rs` (Mac keys and vim keys side by side, Ekin's pick), and `TerminalBody::key` hands it every key while it is on, so nothing reaches the program. Shift+move selects the Mac's way and a plain move drops that selection; `v` / `V` / Ctrl+V start vim's, which plain moves extend. Cmd+C and `y` copy and STAY in the mode; Escape is the only way out (Ekin: leave deliberately). Cmd+Shift+Arrow stays the canvas's (`focus.extend.*`), so select to a line end with Shift+Home/End. The frame's cursor is the visual one, in viewport rows, drawn up in the history too, and the card wears a VISUAL tag.
- The zsh command line selects like a Mac input (2026-09-27), all of it in `shell/infiniterm.zsh` because the shell owns the line: Shift+Left/Right, Shift+Alt+Left/Right and Shift+Home/End (NOT Cmd+Shift+Arrow, which stays the canvas's; Ekin's pick) set zsh's region and switch to an `ift-select` keymap where typing, Backspace, Delete or a paste replace the selection and any other key drops it (zsh-shift-select's way), bound at the first prompt so the user's `.zshrc` cannot reset them. Every change is reported as OSC 52 with the "selection" buffer (`TermEvent::LineSelection`), not the clipboard, so `TerminalBody` can answer Cmd+C (a mouse selection wins) and Cmd+X (copy, then Backspace). Cmd+Backspace / Cmd+Delete send Ctrl+U / Ctrl+K as iTerm2 does, and the integration binds Ctrl+U to readline's to-the-start instead of zsh's whole line. No mouse part and no click-to-move (Ekin declined both). A zpty swallows a typed Ctrl+U as its kill character, so the test reads the binding back.
